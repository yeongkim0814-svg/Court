# The verdict: GUILTY, 6 to 0

**The case:** Make and deploy games and websites to earn money for AI subscription fees and other expenses (a Korean high school senior, year 2026, is the one deciding).

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## How the jury voted

| Juror | Vote | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Lawyer | GUILTY | As a lawyer I see a probable minor (a high school senior in Korea is usually under 19) planning to take payouts, run a business and publish games with no one in the record having checked parental consent, platform age limits, tax or game-rating rules, so it should not go ahead as described. |
| The Small-Business Owner | GUILTY | I would never open a shop with no product, no customer and no idea how the first won comes in, and a senior with exams ahead is the only one to staff it on Monday morning while the 500,000 KRW sits there with nothing to bring it back. |
| The Investor | GUILTY | As an investor I would not fund a plan that names no product, no buyer and no way to earn, where up to 500,000 KRW is at risk against a 1,000,000 KRW target with no stated basis and an unpriced cost in a senior's exam-year hours. |
| The Retired Judge | GUILTY | The burden is on the proponent, and the record holds only a goal and a ceiling on spending, with no product, no revenue route and no basis for the 1,000,000 KRW target, so what is offered is hope, not proof. |
| The Engineer | GUILTY | As an engineer I can build almost anything the AI setup can write, but "games, websites, etc." is not a spec, and what breaks first is not the code but getting users and getting paid, which the plan does not address. |
| The Teenager | GUILTY | "Make games and websites, etc." is not something anyone would talk about, share or come back to next week, because there's no actual thing and no reason for a single person to show up. |

## Charges that stuck
1. No named product, sales channel or basis for the 1,000,000 KRW target (5 jurors).
2. No weekly hour cap or rule that exams come first, so the cost in senior-year hours is unpriced (4 jurors).
3. Nobody has checked whether a minor can legally receive payouts, or the tax and age rules (3 jurors).

## What the defense got right
1. The 500,000 KRW is a ceiling, not a commitment, so the money at risk can stay small.
2. The Claude, Codex and Gemini setup lowers the cost of building and reviewing, though finding customers remains the hard part.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. Pick one product and one sales channel aimed at a defined group (for example other Korean students), with a revenue estimate behind the 1,000,000 KRW.
2. Set a fixed weekly hour cap that puts exams first, plus a stop-loss well below 500,000 KRW and a small first spend.
3. Confirm your legal age and a working payout route (for example a guardian-held account), including tax duties, before any launch.

## The sentence
The plan is unready, not hopeless. Today it is a wish. This week, write one page: the product, the buyer, the channel, your weekly hours and how you will get paid. Talk to a parent or guardian about payouts. Spend nothing until that page exists, then bring it back.
