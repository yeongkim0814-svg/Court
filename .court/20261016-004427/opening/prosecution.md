# Prosecution opening

1. **The plan has no revenue mechanism.** The case says only "building and deploying games, websites, etc." It names no product, audience, channel, price or monetization method (ads, sales, subscriptions). Building is not earning. Cost if I am right: months of work end at 0 KRW, with no way to tell why, because nothing was specified to test.

2. **The target is stated without a path to it.** "Target profit: 1,000,000 KRW" comes with no estimate of traffic, conversion or units sold. Since "profitability comes first," the plan should be built on those numbers, and none are in the record. Cost: the user cannot tell whether the goal is realistic, and cannot tell when to stop.

3. **The budget is all downside and the time is the hidden cost.** "Budget: up to 500,000 KRW may be spent" is half the profit target. Reaching 1,000,000 KRW profit means earning roughly 1,500,000 KRW if all of it is spent. The record sets no spending rule, no stop-loss and no milestone. Cost: up to 500,000 KRW lost, plus unpriced hours.

4. **The window conflicts with the user's real priority.** The period runs "through the winter break, until university entrance," and the user is "a high school senior planning to study physics." The record gives no weekly hour limit and no rule that exam preparation wins. Cost: a side project that competes with the one outcome that matters for this user, entrance.

5. **The strength cited does not close the gap.** "Claude, Codex and Gemini collaborate on and review each other's work" addresses producing code. The user is "not a professional developer," and the record says nothing about marketing, distribution, payment setup, or legal and tax questions for a high school student earning money. Three AI reviewers can ship a polished product that nobody finds. Cost: effort goes into the part that is already cheap, while the part that decides revenue stays unaddressed.

Missing from the record: the product idea, the sales channel, any prior revenue evidence, the user's weekly hours, and whether a minor can legally receive payouts.
