# The verdict: GUILTY, 5 to 1

**The case:** Make game assets (a PS1/PSX-style graphics toolkit for Godot 4 called "RetroForge") and sell them in the Godot store, to raise money for AI subscriptions and other expenses.

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## How the jury voted

| Juror | Vote | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Parent | NOT GUILTY | As a parent I see a teenager risking at most 500,000 KRW and mostly their own spare time, with every open question (store, payout, guardian consent) written into the plan as an approval step, so the plan is risky but not reckless. |
| The Small-Business Owner | GUILTY | As a shop owner I would not open the doors when I don't know which store I'm selling in, whether it will let me sell or pay me, or whether the one person doing all the work can even run the tools, and a $9.99 add-on competing with $5 and free rivals has no shown path to 1,000,000 KRW. |
| The Retired Judge | GUILTY | The case asks me to accept that 1,000,000 KRW is reachable, but the record holds hope rather than proof on the sales channel, the payout route for a minor, paid demand and whether the product can even be built and tested, and the defense itself concedes each of these. |
| The First-Time Customer | GUILTY | As a buyer I can already get a free PSX shader or a paid one for $4.99 to $5.99, so I would not pay about $9.99 for a first release from someone with no track record, and the plan itself admits it has no evidence anyone will pay. |
| The Accountant | GUILTY | I follow the money and cannot find who pays, how much or when: the store, its fees and the payout route for a minor are all unverified, and nothing in the record links the 1,000,000 KRW target to a number of buyers at $4.99 to $9.99. |
| The Competitor | GUILTY | As a rival seller I would ignore this, because a free Lite tier and a $9.99 Pro from a first-time builder cannot beat a free pay-what-you-want PSX shader and $4.99 to $5.99 packs, and I could copy any feature in a weekend. |

## Charges that stuck
1. No confirmed place to sell and no confirmed way for a minor to be paid: the store's rules, fees and payout terms are unchecked, and age and guardian consent are unstated (5 jurors).
2. A $9.99 first release faces free and $4.99-$5.99 rivals, and the plan admits it has no proof anyone will pay (5 jurors).
3. Nothing links the 1,000,000 KRW goal to a number of buyers after fees (3 jurors).

## What the defense got right
1. The loss is capped: at most 500,000 KRW, and the plan requires approval before any spending, publishing, account creation, or guardian-checked payout.
2. The product is narrow and the plan has gates (10 conversations, 30 downloads, 3 willingness-to-pay), so demand can be tested cheaply before money is spent. The problems with Termux, the 14-day schedule and USD prices are fixable edits.

## Conditions for acquittal
1. Name the exact store. Show in writing that it allows paid items from a minor, its fee, and a payout route the user or a consenting guardian can use. Get the guardian's agreement.
2. Prove demand at $9.99. Find a feature rivals lack, then get a few real people to say they would pay for it over the cheaper options.
3. Do the arithmetic: net price after fees, the number of sales needed for 1,000,000 KRW, and whether that is plausible. Also install Godot in the container and confirm the shaders run.

## The sentence
The court does not permit this to proceed as described. Do not build or spend yet. This week, settle the store, the payout and your guardian's consent, and talk to potential buyers. Bring the answers back, and a revised plan may be tried again.
